Five displacements, one architecture

03 / Brake horsepower

Why the old numbers look bigger

Until 1971, Ford advertised SAE gross horsepower: an engine on a stand with no accessories, no exhaust system, and no air cleaner. From 1972, ratings were SAE net — measured as installed. The two are not comparable.

Gross ≠ net
never mix the two

Gross (SAE J245 / J607). No water pump, alternator, power steering, air cleaner, or production exhaust. Optimal timing and jetting. Great for bench racing, useless for predicting what the engine makes in the car.

Net (SAE J1349). Full production accessories, air cleaner, exhaust manifolds, and production timing. This is the number that moved the car.

A 1969 351W rated at 290 gross made roughly 210–220 net in the same state of tune. A 1987 5.0 HO rated at 225 net was genuinely stronger than a 1970 302 rated at 220 gross.

06 / Assembly

Building a 302, in order

A stock-ish 302 rebuild, written for someone doing it for the first time. Work top to bottom, tick each stage as you finish, and don't skip the measuring. Check every number against the manual for your block and hardware.

10 stages · stock 302

Build progress
0 / 10
01 Teardown and inspectionBag it · label it · measure it
  1. Drain the coolant and oil, then pull the intake, heads, and accessories. Keep the pushrods in order — they wear into their lifters.
  2. Number every main cap, rod cap, and rod before removing it. Caps are matched and directional; swapping them ruins the bore.
  3. Strip the block bare and hot-tank or pressure-wash it. Remove all core plugs, the cam bearings, and the oil gallery plugs so the machine shop can clean the passages.
  4. Magnuflux or crack-test the block, crank, and rods. Check the main saddles for line-bore truth before spending money on machine work.
Bore spacing 4.380 inDeck 8.206 inMain journal 2.249 inRod journal 2.123 in

Why it matters. A cracked block or a bent rod is cheaper to find now than after you have bought pistons and machine work.

02 Machine workBore · hone · deck · balance
  1. Have the block bored and honed to the piston maker's clearance — not to a generic number. Torque plates should be on the block during honing.
  2. Deck the block to a true surface and confirm the deck height you actually have. Removing material changes compression and piston-to-valve clearance.
  3. Line-hone or line-bore the mains only if the saddles are out of true or you are using aftermarket caps.
  4. Balance the crank, rods, pistons, damper, and flywheel as a set. Install new cam bearings and core plugs after the block comes back clean.
Piston-to-wall 0.0018–0.0026 in castVerify ring gaps with the ring maker's cardDeck height measured, not assumed

Why it matters. Machine work sets every clearance downstream. If the bore or deck is wrong, no torque value or gasket will save the build.

03 Crankshaft and main bearingsClearance · end play · cap order
  1. Fit the main bearings dry, then measure oil clearance with a micrometer and bore gauge or Plastigauge. Factory target is 0.0004–0.0015 in; up to 0.0021 in is the allowable limit.
  2. Lubricate the journals and bearing faces with assembly lube, lay the crank in, and check that it spins freely by hand.
  3. Torque the caps in the factory sequence — 3-2-4-1-5, center outward — in two or three equal passes to 60–70 ft-lb.
  4. Check crankshaft end play with a dial indicator and confirm it against the manual before the rear main seal goes in.
Main clearance 0.0004–0.0015 inAllowable 0.0021 inEnd play 0.004–0.008 inMain bolts 60–70 ft-lb
Torque order 3-2-4-1-5. Amber is the first cap, cyan next, dim last. Two or three equal passes.
Top row 9-5-1-3-7, bottom row 10-6-2-4-8. Start at the center and spiral out; torque in 2–3 equal steps.

Why it matters. Main clearance and end play decide whether the crank lives. The center-out sequence seats the cap evenly instead of cocking it.

04 Rods and pistonsRing gaps · orientation · rod bolts
  1. Check ring end gaps in the bore they will run in, using the ring maker's spec. File gaps square — never chamfer the ends.
  2. Fit the rings with the correct side up and the gaps staggered. Oil the bores and ring lands before assembly.
  3. Orient each piston so the notch or dot faces the front of the engine, and make sure the rod's oil squirt hole faces the correct side.
  4. Lubricate the rod bearings, pull the pistons in with a tapered ring compressor, and torque rod bolts to 22–25 ft-lb (40–45 for HiPo/Boss rods) in two passes.
  5. Check rod bearing oil clearance (0.0008–0.0015 in) and rod side clearance (typically 0.010–0.020 in) — side clearance is measured between the rod and the crank cheek, not at the bearing.
Rod bolts 22–25 ft-lbRod oil clearance 0.0008–0.0015 inRod side clearance 0.010–0.020 in

Why it matters. A ring installed upside down or a rod facing the wrong way will not seal and cannot be fixed without pulling the engine again.

05 Camshaft and timingDegree it · lube it · line it up
  1. Slide the cam in with plenty of break-in lube on the lobes and distributor gear. Never bang it through a bearing.
  2. Fit the thrust plate and torque to 8–10 ft-lb, then check cam end play.
  3. Install the timing set dot-to-dot and degree the cam with a wheel if you have one. Dot-to-dot is the baseline, not a guarantee.
  4. Torque the cam sprocket bolt to 40–45 ft-lb, then recheck the dots after the bolt is tight.
Cam sprocket 40–45 ft-lbThrust plate 8–10 ft-lbVerify cam end play

Why it matters. A cam that is one tooth off will idle badly, make no vacuum, and can bend valves. Degree it once and know.

06 Oil systemPump · pickup · pan · prime
  1. Install the oil pump with a new driveshaft and torque the bolt to 23–28 ft-lb. Check that the shaft engages the distributor gear fully.
  2. Bolt the pickup to the pump and verify 1/4–3/8 in of clearance to the pan floor. Tack-weld the pickup if the maker allows it.
  3. Fit the pan with a new gasket and sealant at the corners. Torque 1/4-in bolts to 7–9 ft-lb and 5/16-in to 9–11 ft-lb.
  4. Prime the system with a drill-driven pump shaft or a pressurized primer until oil reaches the rockers.
Oil pump 23–28 ft-lbPan 7–11 ft-lb5 qt with filter, stock 302

Why it matters. A pickup resting on the pan floor starves the pump; one sitting too high can uncover on a hard launch. Prime before the first crank.

07 Cylinder headsGaskets · sequence · TTY warning
  1. Clean the deck and head surfaces to bare metal. Check head flatness and the gasket's orientation — "front" and "up" marks matter.
  2. Chase the head bolt holes and blow them out. Oil or sealant on the threads per the manual; never leave fluid pooled in a blind hole.
  3. Torque in the factory sequence — top row 9-5-1-3-7, bottom row 10-6-2-4-8 — in two or three equal steps to 65–72 ft-lb.
  4. If the engine has flanged torque-to-yield bolts: 25–35, then 45–55 ft-lb, then 85–95° more. Do not reuse TTY bolts.
Head bolts 65–72 ft-lb351W 90–100 ft-lbTTY: single-use

Why it matters. Head bolts clamp the gasket, and the sequence is what keeps the head from warping as it loads. Mixing bolt styles on one head leaks.

08 ValvetrainPreload · lash · pushrods
  1. Install the lifters with lube on the faces, then drop the pushrods into their original positions.
  2. Set hydraulic lifter preload per the lifter maker — typically about 1/2 to 1 turn past zero lash on an adjustable valvetrain.
  3. For a solid-lifter 289 HiPo, set lash to the cam card, usually around 0.018–0.020 in hot. Verify before you lock it down.
  4. Torque rocker nuts to 17–23 ft-lb, then turn the engine through two full revolutions by hand and watch for coil bind or retainer contact.
Rocker nuts 17–23 ft-lbHydraulic preload ≈ ½–1 turnHiPo lash per cam card

Why it matters. Wrong preload or lash shows up as noise, lost lift, or a bent pushrod. Two hand revolutions catch interference before the starter does.

09 Intake, ignition and accessoriesSeal · timing · torque
  1. Dry-fit the intake, check port alignment, then seal the ends with RTV and the side gaskets per the maker. Torque to 23–25 ft-lb, center out.
  2. Install the distributor with the engine at TDC on cylinder 1 and the rotor pointing to the number-1 tower. Confirm the firing order.
  3. Set base timing to the underhood label — commonly 10° BTDC for a carbureted 302, with the spout disconnected on EFI. Confirm plug gap.
  4. Torque the damper to 70–90 ft-lb, water pump to 12–18, valve covers to 3–5, and the thermostat housing to 12–15.
Intake 23–25 ft-lbBase timing ≈ 10° BTDCPlug gap ≈ 0.034 in carb / 0.052–0.056 EFI

Why it matters. An intake that leaks at the ends runs lean and whistles; a distributor one tooth off will not time in. Both are easy to get right now.

10 First start and break-inPrime · cam · recheck
  1. Fill with oil and coolant, prime the oil system, and check for leaks before you crank. Confirm the ignition and fuel are live.
  2. Start it and bring it to 1,500–2,000 rpm immediately for flat-tappet cam break-in — about 20–30 minutes, varying the rpm. Do not let it idle.
  3. Watch oil pressure, coolant temperature, and for unusual noise. Shut down immediately if pressure is low or something is knocking.
  4. Cool down, change the oil and filter, then re-torque the intake and exhaust fasteners and recheck timing. Recheck valve lash after the first few heat cycles.
Cam break-in 1,500–2,000 rpm · 20–30 minOil pressure hot: per manualChange oil after break-in

Why it matters. A flat-tappet cam relies on splash and rpm to break in. Idling a fresh engine is how lobes get wiped.

Firing order and quick lookup

Cylinder numbering is the same across the family: passenger side 1-2-3-4 front to rear, driver side 5-6-7-8. The firing order is not — the 5.0 HO and every 351W use the later order.

Passenger side 1-2-3-4
Driver side 5-6-7-8

Standard Windsor

1-5-4-2-6-3-7-8
221, 260, 289, 302 · most carbureted

HO / 351W

1-3-7-2-6-5-4-8
5.0 HO, 351W · check the cam card

The 351W uses the later order because of its different crank and cam layout. A 302 with an HO cam also uses 1-3-7-2-6-5-4-8 — the order follows the camshaft, not the block. If you did not build it, read the cam card or verify with the valve events.
